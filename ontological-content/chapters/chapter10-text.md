Welcome to what might be the most practical chapter in this entire guide. If you're in the asphalt business, you live and die by your equipment. ## 10.1 Site Preparation Equipment

Before you ever lay down asphalt, you need proper site preparation. The foundation of any successful asphalt project is the subgrade and base, and that means earthwork. Let's talk about the machines that make it happen.

### Excavators

Excavators are your workhorse for site preparation. They dig, load, grade, and move material. For asphalt work, you'll typically see excavators in the 20,000 to 90,000-pound class, though smaller projects might use compact excavators in the 7,000 to 15,000-pound range.

**Types and Sizes**

The size of excavator you need depends on your typical project scale:

- **Mini/Compact Excavators (7,000-15,000 lbs)**: Perfect for residential driveways, small parking lots, and confined spaces. They can fit through gates and work in tight areas. Great for utility work and localized repairs.

- **Mid-Size Excavators (20,000-50,000 lbs)**: The sweet spot for most commercial paving contractors. They have enough reach and bucket capacity to move serious material but aren't overkill for typical parking lots and light commercial sites.

- **Large Excavators (50,000-90,000+ lbs)**: For heavy commercial work, road construction, and large-scale site development. These machines move massive amounts of material quickly.

**Bucket Attachments**

Your excavator is only as versatile as your bucket selection:

- **Digging Buckets**: Standard buckets for excavation. Size them based on the excavator's capacity—too large and you can't fill it, too small and you're inefficient.

- **Grading Buckets**: Wider buckets with a flat edge for finish grading and spreading base material. Essential for preparing sub-base before paving.

- **Cleanup Buckets**: Extra-wide buckets for moving loose material like gravel or recycled asphalt.

- **Slope Buckets**: Tiltable buckets for precise grading work on slopes and swales.

**Applications in Grading**

In asphalt work, excavators handle:
- Removing old pavement and base material
- Excavating to design grade
- Loading trucks with spoil material
- Placing and spreading base material
- Utility trench excavation
- Fine grading with grading buckets

**Why This Matters to Customers**: When you're bidding a project and you can say, "We'll be using a 45,000-pound excavator with a 48-inch grading bucket for precise sub-base preparation, which ensures proper pavement support and reduces the risk of settlement," you're speaking the language of quality. Property owners understand that proper preparation prevents future problems.

### Bulldozers

Bulldozers (often called "dozers" or "track-type tractors") are pushing machines. They move material across the ground, spread it, grade it, and compact it—all in one pass.

**Track-Type Tractors**

Most dozers used in asphalt site prep are track-type machines ranging from 20,000 to 100,000 pounds. For paving contractors, the 50,000 to 80,000-pound range is most common. Tracks give you:
- Excellent flotation on soft ground
- Superior pushing power
- Good grading capability
- Built-in compaction from track weight

**Blade Configurations**

The blade is everything on a dozer:

- **Straight Blade (S-Blade)**: Flat blade good for fine grading and spreading material in thinner lifts. Most versatile for paving prep work.

- **Universal Blade (U-Blade)**: Curved blade with side wings. Moves more material per pass—excellent for heavy pushing and large-scale grading.

- **Angle Blade (A-Blade)**: Can be angled left or right to push material to the side. Great for windrow work and side-casting.

**Grading and Spreading**

For asphalt projects, dozers excel at:
- Spreading base material across large areas
- Rough grading to bring site to approximate design elevation
- Pushing and shaping aggregate stockpiles
- Backfilling and compacting fill areas
- Breaking up and pushing out milled asphalt

**Customer Value**: Dozers are efficient. When you're bidding a large parking lot that needs 8 inches of base material, being able to say, "We'll spread and rough-grade the base with a dozer, which allows us to place and grade large quantities efficiently, saving time and reducing costs," shows you understand project economics.

### Motor Graders

Motor graders are precision machines. While excavators dig and dozers push, graders finesse. They're the difference between rough grading and finish grading.

**Fine Grading Capabilities**

A motor grader has a long wheelbase with a blade mounted between the front and rear axles. This design allows for extremely precise grade control—we're talking about accuracy to within a quarter-inch over long distances. For asphalt work, graders are used primarily for:
- Final sub-base preparation before paving
- Maintaining unpaved roads
- Fine-tuning grade and cross-slope
- Creating precise drainage channels

**Blade Control Systems**

Modern graders have sophisticated blade control systems:
- Manual controls for operator skill-based grading
- Automatic grade control using lasers or GPS for precision work
- Articulating blades that can angle, tilt, and shift

**Sub-Base Preparation**

Here's where graders shine in asphalt work: preparing the sub-base. After your excavator has excavated and your dozer has rough-graded, the grader comes in to fine-tune everything. It creates the exact crown, slope, and elevation needed for proper drainage and pavement performance.

**When to Use a Grader**: Graders are expensive machines, and not every paving contractor owns one. For small projects (residential driveways, small parking lots), you can often do fine grading with an excavator and grading bucket. For larger commercial projects and road work, a grader is essential for achieving the precision needed.

**Customer Communication**: When bidding municipal or DOT work, specifications often require motor grader work for sub-base prep. Being able to say, "We'll finish-grade the sub-base with a laser-guided motor grader to achieve the specified grade and cross-slope tolerances," shows you can meet stringent specifications.

## 10.2 Base and Compaction Equipment

Once material is placed, it needs to be compacted. Proper compaction of subgrade and base is absolutely critical to pavement performance. Under-compacted base causes settlement, rutting, and premature pavement failure. Let's talk about the equipment that gets compaction done right.

### Compaction Equipment Types

Different materials and situations require different types of compaction equipment. Understanding when to use which type is key to efficient, high-quality work.

#### Vibratory Rollers

Vibratory rollers use vibration to increase compaction efficiency. They're the workhorse of base compaction for asphalt projects.

**Smooth Drum Rollers**

Smooth drum vibratory rollers are what you'll use most for compacting aggregate base under asphalt pavements. Key features:

- **Single Drum Rollers**: One large vibratory drum at the front, pneumatic tires at the rear. Great for base work because the drum compacts while the tires knead and seal.

- **Double Drum Rollers (Tandem Rollers)**: Vibratory drums at both front and rear. More aggressive compaction, commonly used for base preparation and some asphalt compaction.

- **Weight Range**: Base compaction rollers typically range from 5 to 15 tons. Heavier is better for thicker lifts and larger aggregate.

**How Vibration Works**: The drum contains an eccentric weight that rotates, creating a rapid up-and-down motion. This vibration helps aggregate particles settle into a dense configuration. Most rollers offer:
- High-frequency (3,000-4,000 VPM) for shallow lifts and finishing
- Low-frequency (2,000-3,000 VPM) for deeper lifts and initial compaction

**Padfoot Rollers**

Padfoot rollers have protruding feet or cleats on the drum. These feet penetrate the material, breaking up clods and compacting from the bottom up. Padfoot rollers are excellent for:
- Compacting cohesive soils (clays and silts) in subgrade preparation
- Breaking up oversized clumps
- Working in deeper lifts (12+ inches)

For asphalt base work, you'll rarely use padfoot rollers on aggregate base (they'd damage the surface), but they're valuable for subgrade compaction when you're working with native soils.

**Single and Double Drum Configurations**

The choice between single and double drum comes down to application:
- **Single Drum**: Better for base work where you want aggressive compaction from the drum but a smooth finish from the tires
- **Double Drum**: More uniform compaction, better for thinner lifts, and when you need to work both forward and reverse without changing compaction effectiveness

#### Pneumatic Tire Rollers

Pneumatic tire rollers use rubber tires instead of steel drums. They provide a different type of compaction—a kneading action rather than static or vibratory force.

**Kneading Action**

The rubber tires flex and knead the material as they roll over it. This action:
- Works material particles together rather than just pressing down
- Provides compaction through the full depth of the lift
- Is less likely to over-compact or crush aggregate particles

**Uniform Compaction**

Pneumatic rollers typically have 7 to 13 tires arranged in two rows, with the rear tires offset to cover the gaps left by the front tires. This provides complete coverage across the roller width.

**Surface Sealing**

One key benefit of pneumatic rollers: they seal the surface. The rubber tires press fine particles into voids, creating a tight surface that resists water infiltration. For aggregate base under asphalt, this sealing action is valuable because it:
- Reduces moisture penetration
- Creates a stable surface for the paver to roll on
- Helps prevent pumping of fines during asphalt placement

**When to Use Pneumatic Rollers**: They're excellent for finish compaction of aggregate base, especially when you want a sealed, stable surface. Many contractors use a combination: initial compaction with a vibratory roller, finish compaction with a pneumatic roller.

#### Plate Compactors

Plate compactors (also called vibrating plates) are hand-guided machines for small areas. They have a flat steel plate that vibrates at high frequency.

**Small Area Applications**

Plate compactors excel where larger equipment can't reach:
- Around building foundations
- Adjacent to walls and curbs
- In landscaped areas
- Around light poles and signs

**Trench and Utility Work**

After utility companies cut through your pavement for water lines, gas lines, or electrical work, someone has to compact that backfill. Plate compactors are the standard equipment for trench compaction in 6-inch lifts.

**Hand-Guided Operation**

Plate compactors range from small (300-500 pounds, single-direction) to large (1,000+ pounds, reversible). For asphalt site work, the mid-size range (400-700 pounds) is most versatile.

**Customer Value**: When you're bidding a parking lot with landscaping islands, curbs, and light poles, mentioning that you'll use plate compactors for proper compaction in areas the roller can't reach demonstrates thoroughness.

#### Trench Rollers

Trench rollers are walk-behind vibratory rollers specifically designed for utility trenches. They're narrower than standard rollers (24 to 36 inches wide) and can work in confined spaces.

**Walk-Behind Units**

The operator walks behind the machine, guiding it through the trench. Most units are:
- Single drum design with vibration
- Remote-controlled or handle-guided
- Water-cooled or air-cooled diesel engines

**Confined Space Compaction**

For deep utility trenches or areas too confined for plate compactors, trench rollers provide the best compaction. They're essential for:
- Utility repair compaction in roads
- Backfilling trenches in parking lots
- Compacting base in narrow areas

### Compaction Attachments

Modern construction efficiency comes from versatility. Rather than owning a dedicated piece of equipment for every task, many contractors use attachments.

**Excavator-Mounted Plates**

Compaction plates that attach to excavator buckets allow you to compact while excavating. Benefits:
- Compact trench bottom before backfilling
- Compact lifts as you backfill
- Reduce the need for separate compaction equipment on small jobs

**Roller Wheels for Excavators**

Wheel-type compaction attachments that mount to excavators can compact soil and base in confined spaces. The excavator provides the weight and vibration control.

**Versatility Enhancement**

For smaller contractors, compaction attachments can reduce equipment costs. One excavator with a compaction plate attachment can handle excavation and compaction, eliminating the need to own and transport a dedicated compactor for smaller jobs.

**Why Equipment Matters to Customers**: Under-compacted base is the number one cause of premature pavement failure. When you're explaining your process to a customer and you say, "We compact the base in 6-inch lifts with a 10-ton vibratory roller, and use plate compactors along the edges and around obstacles to ensure 95% compaction throughout," you're demonstrating that you understand the importance of proper foundation work. That builds confidence.

## 10.3 Asphalt Production and Transport

Quality asphalt pavement starts at the plant. Understanding production and transport equipment helps you work better with your suppliers, troubleshoot problems, and explain quality control to customers.

### Asphalt Plants

Asphalt plants mix aggregate, binder, and any additives at controlled temperatures to produce hot mix asphalt. There are two main types.

**Batch Plants**

Batch plants produce asphalt in individual batches (typically 2 to 6 tons per batch). The process:
1. Aggregate is heated and dried in a rotating drum
2. Hot aggregate is elevated to storage bins
3. A predetermined amount of each aggregate size is weighed and dropped into a mixer (called a pugmill)
4. Asphalt binder is added and everything is mixed for 30-60 seconds
5. The finished batch is discharged into a waiting truck

**Advantages of Batch Plants**:
- Precise control over each batch composition
- Easy to change mix designs between batches
- Good for projects requiring multiple mix types
- Common in regions with varied mix requirements

**Drum Mix Plants**

Drum mix plants (also called drum plants or continuous plants) mix everything in a single rotating drum. The process:
1. Aggregate is fed continuously into the drum
2. Aggregate is dried and heated as it tumbles toward the discharge end
3. Asphalt binder is sprayed into the drum
4. Mixing occurs as materials tumble together in the drum
5. Hot mix continuously discharges into a storage silo or directly into trucks

**Advantages of Drum Mix Plants**:
- Higher production rates (200-500 tons per hour vs. 150-300 for batch)
- Lower capital cost
- Simpler operation
- Good for large, single-mix projects

**Mobile/Portable Plants**

Both batch and drum plants can be mobile (mounted on trailers for road transport) or portable (can be disassembled and moved but require more time). Mobile plants are valuable for:
- Remote projects far from permanent plants
- Large projects where on-site production reduces trucking costs
- Temporary project-specific installations

**Production Capacity Ranges**

Plant capacity matters for project planning:
- Small plants: 150-200 tons per hour
- Medium plants: 250-400 tons per hour
- Large plants: 400-600+ tons per hour

**Why This Matters**: When you're bidding a large project that requires 5,000 tons of asphalt, and the nearest plant can produce 300 tons per hour, you know you need two days of production at minimum. This affects your project scheduling and pricing.

### Dump Trucks

Getting hot mix from the plant to the job site quickly and without temperature loss is critical. That's where dump trucks come in.

**Insulated Truck Bodies**

Standard dump trucks lose heat rapidly. For asphalt transport, insulated bodies or tarps are essential:
- Insulated bodies maintain mix temperature during transport
- Reduces "cold load" problems when mix arrives too cool to compact properly
- Extends the time asphalt remains workable

**Capacity Ratings**

Asphalt trucks typically range from:
- Small trucks: 8-12 tons (single-axle or tandem)
- Medium trucks: 15-20 tons (tri-axle)
- Large trucks: 22-25 tons (tri-axle or quad-axle)

Truck capacity affects your paving efficiency. Larger trucks mean:
- Fewer truck cycles needed
- Less time with the paver waiting
- Better paving continuity

**Material Protection During Transport**

Beyond insulation, proper transport means:
- Tarp covering to prevent heat loss and moisture infiltration
- Clean truck beds (release agent applied) to prevent sticking
- Proper loading to prevent segregation (segregation occurs when coarse aggregate separates from fine during loading)

**Customer Communication**: Explaining transport logistics shows professionalism. "We use insulated, tarped trucks and schedule loads to arrive at 12-minute intervals, ensuring continuous paving without cold joints or delays" demonstrates operational planning.

## 10.4 Paving Equipment

This is where asphalt contractors make their reputation: the paving operation. Your paver and how you use it directly determine the quality of the finished surface.

### Asphalt Pavers

Modern asphalt pavers are sophisticated machines designed to receive hot mix asphalt, spread it uniformly, and provide initial compaction to create a smooth, dense mat ready for final rolling.

**Tracked Pavers**

Tracked pavers ride on rubber tracks (similar to an excavator). Benefits:
- Excellent traction for pushing heavy loads of mix
- Superior flotation—less likely to sink into soft base
- Better stability for smooth, consistent paving
- Preferred for highway and road work

Most tracked pavers used in commercial and municipal paving range from 8 to 16 feet in width (extendable).

**Wheeled Pavers**

Wheeled pavers ride on rubber tires (usually four-wheel or six-wheel drive). Benefits:
- More maneuverable for tight spaces
- Easier to transport (just drive onto a trailer)
- Lower initial cost
- Good for parking lots and lighter commercial work

**Width Capabilities**

Modern pavers offer remarkable width flexibility:
- Base width: typically 8 to 12 feet
- Extendable to: 12 to 16 feet with bolt-on extensions
- Some large highway pavers: up to 30 feet with specialized screeds

For commercial paving contractors, a paver with a 10-foot base width extendable to 14 or 15 feet covers most jobs.

**Hopper Capacity**

The hopper (receiving bin at the front of the paver) holds mix dumped from trucks. Larger hoppers mean:
- Trucks can dump faster
- Better surge capacity when trucks arrive bunched together
- More consistent paving when truck delivery is irregular

Typical hopper capacity: 10 to 15 tons.

**Screed Types**

The screed is the heart of the paver. It shapes, strikes off, and initially compacts the mix to create the pavement mat.

**Fixed-Width Screeds**: Non-extending screeds for consistent-width paving.

**Extending Screeds**: Hydraulically extend to increase paving width. Extensions can be:
- Manual bolt-on extensions
- Hydraulic power extensions (extend and retract on the fly)

**Adjustable Screeds**: Modern screeds offer:
- Heat control (electric or gas heating to keep the screed at proper temperature)
- Vibration control (frequency and amplitude adjustable for different mixes)
- Automatic grade and slope control (using ski sensors or GPS/laser)
- Crown control for creating center crowns on roads

**Why Paver Selection Matters**: Different projects need different pavers. A residential driveway contractor doesn't need a $500,000 tracked paver. But a contractor bidding municipal street work does need a paver with grade control capabilities and sufficient width. Being able to tell a customer, "We use a GPS-guided, 13-foot tracked paver with automatic grade control, ensuring your parking lot meets the specified slopes for proper drainage" demonstrates capability.

### Material Transfer Vehicles (MTVs)

Material Transfer Vehicles are game-changers for quality asphalt paving, yet many smaller contractors don't know about them or understand their value.

**Remix and Surge Capabilities**

An MTV sits between the dump truck and the paver. The truck dumps into the MTV, and the MTV feeds the paver. Inside the MTV:
- A conveyor or auger remixes the material, eliminating segregation
- The MTV acts as a surge hopper, allowing the paver to run continuously even when truck delivery is irregular

**Temperature Maintenance**

MTVs are insulated and often heated, maintaining mix temperature during the transfer process. This is critical for:
- Extending paving time on larger projects
- Preventing cold spots in the mix
- Ensuring uniform compaction

**Segregation Prevention**

Segregation—when coarse aggregate separates from fine material—is a major quality problem. It causes:
- Weak spots in the pavement
- Rough texture
- Poor compaction
- Premature failure

MTVs virtually eliminate segregation by remixing the material before it enters the paver.

**When to Use an MTV**: For small projects (driveways, small lots), an MTV is overkill. For larger commercial projects, municipal work, and any job where uniformity is critical, an MTV is worth the added cost. Many highway specifications now require MTV use.

**Customer Value**: "We use a material transfer vehicle for projects over 500 tons to eliminate segregation and ensure uniform quality across the entire pavement" is a powerful statement of quality commitment.

### Specialty Paving Equipment

Beyond standard pavers, there's equipment for specific applications.

**Windrow Elevators**

Windrow elevators pick up asphalt placed in windrows (long piles) and feed it into the paver. Used primarily in road construction where asphalt is dumped from trucks into windrows along the edge of the road, then picked up and paved continuously.

**Shoulder Pavers**

Smaller pavers designed specifically for paving road shoulders. They can:
- Pave narrow widths (3 to 8 feet)
- Operate adjacent to existing pavement
- Create precise slopes for drainage

**Small Lot Pavers**

Compact pavers designed for:
- Residential driveways
- Small parking areas
- Pathways and trails
- Confined spaces

These machines are more maneuverable and economical for small-scale work.

## 10.5 Compaction and Finishing Equipment

Paving doesn't end when the paver rolls by. Compaction is where you achieve density, and density is everything. Properly compacted asphalt lasts; under-compacted asphalt fails prematurely.

### Asphalt Rollers

Asphalt rollers are specialized compactors designed for compacting hot asphalt. They're different from base compaction rollers—lighter weight, smoother drums, and designed not to damage the fresh mat.

**Static Steel Wheel Rollers**

Static rollers compact purely through their weight—no vibration. They're often used as:
- Breakdown rollers (first pass after the paver)
- Finish rollers (final pass to smooth the surface)

Typical weight range: 3 to 10 tons (for asphalt). Larger static rollers can leave marks and push the mat.

**Vibratory Tandem Rollers**

These are the most common asphalt rollers. Key features:
- Two smooth drums (front and rear)
- Vibration can be turned on or off
- Weight range: 2 to 10 tons for asphalt work

Vibration is used during initial compaction to achieve density faster. It's turned off for finish rolling to avoid over-compaction or creating a wavy surface.

**Pneumatic Tire Rollers**

Pneumatic tire rollers (rubber-tired rollers) are valuable for:
- Intermediate rolling (between breakdown and finish)
- Kneading compaction that seals small surface voids
- Compacting mix that's too cool for vibratory rolling

For asphalt, pneumatic rollers are lighter than those used for base compaction—typically 2 to 8 tons.

**Weight Classes and Specifications**

Roller selection depends on:
- **Mix Type**: SMA and fine-graded mixes need lighter rollers; coarse mixes can handle heavier rollers
- **Lift Thickness**: Thicker lifts require heavier rollers for compaction through the full depth
- **Temperature**: Hot mixes compact more easily and need lighter initial rolling

**Typical Rolling Pattern**:
1. **Breakdown Rolling**: Immediately behind paver, achieve initial compaction (90-92% density). Often done with vibratory roller, vibration on, 2-4 passes.
2. **Intermediate Rolling**: After breakdown, further densify and seal the surface (93-95% density). Often pneumatic tire roller, 2-4 passes.
3. **Finish Rolling**: Final pass to smooth out roller marks. Static steel-wheel roller, vibration off, 1-2 passes.

**Why Rolling Matters to Customers**: Achieving proper density (typically 92-96% of lab maximum density) ensures pavement longevity. When you explain, "We use a three-roller train: breakdown, intermediate, and finish rolling to achieve the specified 94% density," customers understand you're not just running a roller back and forth randomly.

### Hand Tools

Don't underestimate the importance of hand tools. They're how you handle details, edges, and problem spots.

**Lutes and Rakes**

Asphalt lutes (long-handled rakes with straight edges) are used for:
- Spreading mix in areas the paver can't reach
- Leveling small high or low spots
- Working mix around obstacles

Good lutes have:
- Long handles for reach
- Sturdy, straight edges
- Heat-resistant construction

**Hand Tampers**

Hand tampers (also called tamp bars or hand compactors) are heavy steel plates on long handles. They're used to:
- Compact asphalt in confined spaces
- Tamp down edges and transitions
- Compact around structures and curbs

**Shovels and Brooms**

Standard tools with specialized uses in asphalt:
- Square-point shovels for moving and spreading mix
- Flat shovels for scraping and cleaning
- Push brooms for sweeping loose material
- Wire brooms for surface preparation

**Infrared Thermometers**

Non-contact infrared thermometers are essential for quality control:
- Check mix temperature as delivered
- Monitor mat temperature during rolling
- Verify minimum compaction temperatures
- Identify cold spots that need attention

Most asphalt IR thermometers read from 0°F to 500°F with accuracy within ±2°F.

**Why Hand Tools Matter**: Customers don't see hand tools as valuable, but they make the difference between a sloppy-looking job and a professional one. The attention to detail at edges, transitions, and around obstacles shows craftsmanship.

## 10.6 Maintenance and Repair Equipment

The maintenance and repair side of asphalt work requires specialized equipment. This is where smaller contractors can find profitable niches without the capital investment required for paving.

### Milling Machines (Cold Planers)

Milling machines remove asphalt pavement by grinding it off with a rotating drum covered with carbide cutting teeth. The result: a milled surface ready for overlay and recycled asphalt (millings) that can be reused.

**Drum Width Options**

Milling machines range from:
- Small walk-behind planers: 12 to 24 inches wide (for utility cuts, small repairs)
- Mid-size planers: 3 to 6 feet wide (for commercial lots, driveways)
- Large planers: 6 to 13 feet wide (for highway work)

For commercial contractors, a 4-foot-wide planer is versatile.

**Cutting Depth Capabilities**

Milling depth depends on the machine:
- Small machines: up to 4 inches
- Large machines: up to 12+ inches (multiple passes)

Most milling for overlay work is 1.5 to 2 inches—enough to remove the worn surface and create a clean bond surface.

**Dust Control Systems**

Modern milling machines have integrated dust control:
- Water spray systems to suppress dust
- Vacuum systems that capture dust and small millings
- Environmentally friendly operation

**Customer Value**: "We'll mill off the existing 2 inches of worn asphalt, which gives us a clean surface for the new overlay and provides proper elevation at transitions" is more professional than "We'll scrape off some of the old asphalt."

### Crack Sealing Equipment

Crack sealing is preventive maintenance that extends pavement life. Proper crack sealing requires specialized equipment.

**Hot-Pour Kettles**

Hot-pour crack sealant must be heated to 375-400°F for proper application. Kettles range from:
- Small trailer-mounted kettles: 50-100 gallons
- Large truck-mounted kettles: 200-400 gallons

Key features:
- Direct-fired or oil-jacketed heating
- Agitator to prevent material separation
- Temperature control (critical—overheated sealant loses effectiveness)
- Hose reels and wands for application

**Crack Routers**

Routing is the process of cutting a reservoir along the crack to hold more sealant. Router equipment includes:
- Walk-behind routers with carbide cutting blades
- V-shaped or square cutting profiles
- Dust collection systems

Routing isn't always necessary, but it improves sealant adhesion and performance on wider cracks.

**Cleaning Equipment**

Before sealant is applied, cracks must be clean and dry:
- Wire brushes for loosening debris
- Compressed air lances for blowing out cracks (high-pressure, high-volume)
- Heat lances (optional) for drying damp cracks

**Application Wands**

The applicator wand controls sealant flow and placement:
- Trigger control for starting/stopping flow
- Adjustable flow rate
- Various tip sizes for different crack widths

**Why Crack Sealing Matters**: Crack sealing is one of the most cost-effective maintenance activities. When bidding maintenance contracts, being able to say, "We clean, route, and seal all cracks with hot-pour rubberized sealant using professional-grade equipment, which prevents water infiltration and extends pavement life by 3-5 years" demonstrates value.

### Surface Treatment Equipment

Surface treatments (chip seal, slurry seal, seal coating) extend pavement life at a fraction of the cost of overlay or reconstruction.

**Asphalt Distributors (Chip Seal)**

Distributors are trucks equipped with heated tanks and spray bars for applying liquid asphalt. For chip seal:
1. Distributor sprays asphalt emulsion or binder
2. Immediately followed by a truck spreading aggregate chips
3. Roller compacts the chips into the asphalt

Distributors have:
- Heated tanks (3,000-6,000 gallon capacity)
- Adjustable spray bars (8-16 feet wide)
- Precise application rate control
- Computer controls for uniformity

**Slurry Seal Machines**

Slurry seal is a mixture of asphalt emulsion, fine aggregate, mineral filler, and water applied as a thin surface treatment. Slurry machines:
- Mix components on the truck
- Apply through a spreader box at the rear
- Typical application: 1/8 to 3/8 inch thickness

**Seal Coating Spray Systems**

Seal coating (coal tar or asphalt emulsion sealers) is common for parking lots. Equipment:
- Spray systems mounted on trailers or trucks
- Squeegee application for heavy-duty areas
- Two-coat applications typical

**Why Surface Treatments Matter**: For parking lot owners, seal coating every 3-4 years can double pavement life. Being able to offer this as a maintenance service creates recurring revenue and long-term client relationships.

### Pavement Saws

Sawing asphalt and concrete is necessary for:
- Creating clean edges for patches
- Cutting around utility access points
- Saw-cutting joints

**Concrete/Asphalt Saws**

Walk-behind saws range from:
- Small handheld saws (7-14 inch blades) for detail work
- Walk-behind saws (14-30 inch blades) for full-depth cutting
- Self-propelled saws for production cutting

**Diamond Blade Specifications**

Diamond blades are essential for clean cuts:
- Segmented blades for fast cutting
- Continuous rim blades for smooth cuts
- Asphalt-specific blades have softer bonds than concrete blades

**Cut Depth and Width Capabilities**

Most walk-behind saws can cut:
- Depth: 5-12 inches (depending on blade size)
- Some large saws: up to 24+ inches deep

For asphalt repair, a 5-6 inch cut depth is usually sufficient.

## 10.7 Testing and Quality Control Equipment

Quality asphalt work requires testing—both in the field and in the lab. Understanding testing equipment helps you implement quality control and speak intelligently with engineers and inspectors.

### Field Testing Devices

Field testing happens during construction for real-time quality control.

**Nuclear Density Gauges**

Nuclear gauges use radioactive sources to measure density. They're the industry standard for:
- Measuring asphalt density (as percentage of maximum theoretical density)
- Quick results (1-2 minutes per test)
- Non-destructive testing

Nuclear gauges require:
- Licensed operators (radiation safety training)
- Security protocols (they're tightly regulated)
- Annual calibration

**Non-Nuclear Density Gauges**

Due to regulatory concerns with nuclear devices, non-nuclear gauges are gaining popularity:
- Use electromagnetic waves or ground-penetrating radar
- No radiation licensing required
- Comparable accuracy to nuclear gauges

**Core Drilling Equipment**

Core drills extract cylindrical samples of asphalt for lab testing:
- Typical core diameter: 4 or 6 inches
- Full-depth cores for density and thickness verification
- Water-cooled diamond bits
- Portable or truck-mounted rigs

**Infrared Thermometers**

Already mentioned with hand tools, but critical for QC:
- Monitor mat temperature continuously during paving and rolling
- Verify mix meets minimum compaction temperature
- Identify temperature problems before they cause defects

**Moisture Meters**

Moisture meters check for moisture in:
- Aggregate stockpiles before mixing
- Base material before paving
- Existing pavement before overlay

Excess moisture causes:
- Stripping (loss of adhesion between binder and aggregate)
- Poor compaction
- Pavement distress

### Laboratory Equipment

Lab testing provides detailed quality control and mix design verification.

**Superpave Gyratory Compactor**

The Superpave Gyratory Compactor (SGC) is the standard equipment for Superpave mix design:
- Compacts samples under controlled conditions
- Simulates traffic compaction
- Measures density at various gyrations
- Used for volumetric mix design

**Marshall Stability Apparatus**

Marshall testing (older but still used) measures:
- Stability (load-carrying capacity)
- Flow (deformation under load)
- Used to evaluate mix strength

**Ignition Oven**

Ignition ovens determine asphalt content by:
- Burning off the asphalt binder
- Weighing the remaining aggregate
- Calculating binder content by weight loss

**Sieve Shakers**

Sieve analysis determines aggregate gradation:
- Mechanical shakers vibrate stacked sieves
- Separate aggregate into size fractions
- Verify mix gradation meets specification

**Asphalt Binder Testing Equipment**

Testing binder properties requires specialized equipment:
- Rotational viscometer (measures viscosity)
- Dynamic Shear Rheometer (DSR) for high-temperature properties
- Bending Beam Rheometer (BBR) for low-temperature properties
- Used to verify PG grade compliance

**Why Testing Matters**: When you tell a customer, "We perform nuclear density testing at random locations to verify we're achieving 94% density as specified, and we can provide the test results for your records," you're demonstrating commitment to quality and documentation. For commercial and municipal work, testing is often required—having the right equipment and trained technicians sets you apart.
